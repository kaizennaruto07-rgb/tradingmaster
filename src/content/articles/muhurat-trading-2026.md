## Muhurat Trading 2026 at a glance: date, time, and official schedule

- **Date:** Sunday, 8 November 2026 — Diwali Laxmi Pujan, marking the start of Vikram Samvat 2083.
- **Main session:** 2:20 PM – 3:30 PM IST (a 70-minute afternoon session this year, not the traditional evening slot).
- **Official source:** NSE circular NSE/CMTR/76941 (Ref 149/2026) and BSE notice 20261019-9, both dated 19 October 2026. Every timing below comes from these circulars.
- **Who can trade:** anyone with an active demat and trading account; no separate account is needed. Participation is optional.
- **Segments open:** equity, equity futures & options, currency derivatives, commodity derivatives, and securities lending & borrowing (SLB).
- **Settlement:** standard T+1, exactly like a regular trading day.

That's the entire core answer. The rest of this article walks through the full stage-by-stage schedule, explains this year's big change (the Closing Auction Session), and covers what you can and can't do in the 70-minute window.

## What is Muhurat trading?

Muhurat means "auspicious time." Once a year, on Diwali's Laxmi Pujan day, India's stock exchanges hold a short special trading session so investors can mark the new Samvat year — and the Chopda Pujan tradition of opening fresh account ledgers — with a symbolic first trade. Traders and investors buy shares (often in token quantities) as a gesture of prosperity for the year ahead.

The tradition began on the Bombay Stock Exchange in 1957 and was adopted by the NSE in 1992; a companion article in this series covers the full story of Muhurat trading from 1957 to today.

## The official 2026 session schedule, stage by stage

Every timing below is from NSE/CMTR/76941 (Ref 149/2026) and BSE 20261019-9, both dated 19 October 2026. All times are IST.

|---|---|
| Block deal window | 1:50 PM – 2:05 PM |
| Pre-open: order entry (limit + market orders) | 2:05 PM – 2:10 PM |
| Pre-open: order entry (limit orders only) | 2:10 PM – 2:15 PM |
| Pre-open: order matching | 2:15 PM – 2:17 PM |

| Post-close session | 3:50 PM – 4:00 PM |
| Trade modification cut-off | 4:15 PM |

Illiquid scrips trade in periodic call auctions (2:25–3:10 PM) rather than continuous trading.

Read it the way it unfolds. From 1:50 PM the exchanges accept block deals. The pre-open runs from 2:05 to 2:20 PM in four distinct phases — you can enter limit and market orders for the first five minutes, limit orders only for the next five, then orders match between 2:15 and 2:17 PM, followed by a buffer before the main session opens at 2:20 PM. Continuous trading then runs until 3:30 PM for most securities. The post-close window (3:50–4:00 PM) lets you place orders at the closing price, and brokers have until 4:15 PM to modify trades.

## Closing auction explained: why some stocks stop trading at 3:15, not 3:30

This is the newest — and most misunderstood — part of this year's session. The Closing Auction Session (CAS) went live on 3 August 2026 (under a SEBI circular of 16 January 2026), which makes the 8 November 2026 Muhurat session **the first Muhurat session since CAS was introduced**. Here's what it means for your orders.

CAS applies to F&O-linked stocks in the equity cash segment. For those stocks:

1. **Continuous trading ends at 3:15 PM** — 15 minutes before the rest of the cash market.
2. **A closing auction runs from 3:15 to 3:35 PM.** In the auction, orders accumulate within a ±3% band around a reference price, and the equilibrium of that auction sets the stock's **official closing price**.
3. This replaces the old method, where the closing price was a 30-minute volume-weighted average price (VWAP) of trades from the last half hour.

Non-F&O stocks are unaffected for now — they keep trading until 3:30 PM and still settle their closing price on VWAP.

Two things to note. First, if you hold a large-cap stock and see its quote freeze at 3:15 PM on Muhurat day, nothing is broken — that's the closing auction taking over. Second, the circular's values — 3:15 to 3:35 PM for closing auction activities, with CAS transition allowed until 3:50 PM — are the authoritative ones; broker write-ups sometimes paraphrase these timings (for instance, splitting out an order-entry sub-phase).

## How the session actually works: segments, orders, and the 70-minute window

### Which segments are open

Per the exchange circulars, the 2026 Muhurat session covers the equity segment, equity futures & options, currency derivatives, commodity derivatives, and securities lending & borrowing (SLB). So yes — the F&O market is open, which answers the most common derivatives question directly.

### What you can do in the 70-minute window

Operationally, the session works like a compressed regular trading day. You can:

- Place market and limit orders through your normal broker terminal — no separate account or special setup.
- Trade intraday, buy for delivery, or trade F&O contracts within the session window.
- Use the pre-open (2:05–2:20 PM) to place orders before continuous trading begins, and the post-close (3:50–4:00 PM) to transact at the closing price.

### What you can't do

- **You can't carry intraday positions overnight.** Intraday positions are squared off before the session ends; there is no overnight carry.
- **You can't fix mistakes the next morning.** The window is 70 minutes. If you place a wrong order, you have minutes — not hours — to spot and correct it. There's no next regular session that day.
- Normal circuit filters and exchange rules apply throughout, which limits how far any single stock can move in the window.

This is also why realistic expectations matter. A 70-minute session gives a strategy no room to breathe — there's no time for an expectancy edge to express itself, something we cover in depth in our guide to [What Is Trading Expectancy](/articles/what-is-trading-expectancy/).

## Trades are real, not symbolic

This deserves emphasis because the symbolism can mislead: **every Muhurat trade is a real trade with real settlement obligations.** Your demat account is debited or credited. T+1 settlement applies exactly as on a normal day. Brokerage and statutory charges — including STT of 0.15% on the sell side for delivery trades — are levied on every trade, including the token symbolic ones.

Treat a ₹1,000 symbolic buy the way you'd treat any other buy: check the charges, confirm the order details, and don't place it at a market price in a thinly traded stock where the spread is wide.

## Settlement after the session: what lands in your demat

Settlement is standard T+1, identical to a regular trading day. Shares you buy on the Muhurat session are credited to your demat account by T+1 evening; the seller receives funds on T+1. India's T+1 cycle has been fully in force since January 2023, and the Muhurat session is no exception.

The days around the session run like this: Sunday 8 November is the Muhurat session, Monday 9 November is a normal trading day, Tuesday 10 November the market is closed for Diwali-Balipratipada, and trading resumes on Wednesday 11 November.

The detailed mechanics — how T+1 interacts with the holiday gap, what the T1-holding display in your broker's app means, and the full charge breakdown for the session — belong to a companion article in this series on settlement, charges, and the holiday gap. What you need here: the session settles normally, and the only quirk in the calendar is the Tuesday closure.

## Should you buy during Muhurat trading?

The short answer: participation is optional, and the decision is personal rather than financial. Press coverage of this year's announcement has treated the session's significance as traditional and symbolic rather than about immediate profitability — and the compressed window can actually heighten volatility. Seventy minutes of thin, sentiment-driven trading is not a strategy.

If you do participate, treat it as a ceremonial first trade for the new Samvat year: small size, liquid stocks, and full awareness that you're paying real brokerage and charges on the trade. ## Risks and limitations to know before you trade

- **No guaranteed profits.** Nothing about an auspicious day changes a stock's risk. The idea that the session is somehow safer or luckier than any other trading day is a myth — keep your position sizing honest.
- **Lower volume can mean wider spreads.** The session is shorter and thinner than a normal day. Thin order books mean the price you see may not be the price you get, especially in less-liquid stocks. Use limit orders.
- **No room to recover from mistakes.** A fat-fingered order at 3:25 PM leaves you five minutes. In a normal session you'd have the rest of the day.
- **Normal rules apply.** Circuit filters, exchange surveillance, and your broker's risk management are all live. The market won't suspend its safeguards for the occasion.
- **Intraday must square off.** Carry-over doesn't work when the session ends at 3:30 PM — plan your exit before you enter, and know your own ruin threshold before a 70-minute window ever tests it. ## Muhurat Trading 2026 FAQs

### What is Muhurat trading?

Muhurat trading is a special 70-minute trading session held on Diwali's Laxmi Pujan day — this year, Sunday 8 November 2026 — so investors can mark the start of the new Samvat year (Vikram Samvat 2083) with an auspicious first trade. Trading on both NSE and BSE follows the official session schedule, and every trade placed is a real trade with real settlement.

### When is Muhurat trading 2026, and what are the timings?

Muhurat Trading 2026 is on **Sunday, 8 November 2026**. The official schedule (IST), per NSE circular NSE/CMTR/76941 and BSE notice 20261019-9, both dated 19 October 2026:

- Block deals: 1:50 PM – 2:05 PM
- Pre-open: 2:05 PM – 2:20 PM (order entry in phases, matching 2:15 – 2:17 PM)
- Main session: 2:20 PM – 3:30 PM (CAS securities — F&O-linked stocks — stop continuous trading at 3:15 PM)
- Closing auction: 3:15 PM – 3:35 PM
- Post-close: 3:50 PM – 4:00 PM
- Trade modification cut-off: 4:15 PM

This year's session is in the afternoon (2:20–3:30 PM), not the traditional evening slot.

### Is Muhurat trading compulsory?

No. Participation is entirely optional. If you don't want to take part, you simply don't log in — nothing happens to your account, your holdings, or your broker relationship. Only investors who want to place the symbolic first trade of the Samvat year need to be present.

### Do I need a separate demat or trading account for Muhurat trading?

No. Your existing active demat and trading account works for the session. You only need funds in your trading account and to place orders within the session timings — no special registration, separate account, or extra activation is needed.

### Can I trade futures and options during Muhurat trading?

Yes. Equity futures & options are open during the Muhurat session, per the exchange circulars, along with equity, currency derivatives, commodity derivatives, and SLB. Normal F&O rules — margins, contract expiry, and square-off — apply within the session window.

### Does the settlement process differ from a regular trading day?

No. Muhurat trades settle on the standard T+1 cycle: shares bought on the session day are credited to your demat account by T+1 evening, and sellers receive funds on T+1. The calendar around this year's session: Sunday 8 November (Muhurat session), Monday 9 November (normal trading day), Tuesday 10 November (market closed for Diwali-Balipratipada), trading resumes Wednesday 11 November. A companion article in this series covers the settlement mechanics, charges, and the holiday gap in full.

### How much money do I need to participate?

There is no regulatory minimum investment — investors commonly place small token trades to mark the occasion. In practice you're limited by the price of the share you want to buy and your broker's charges. Normal circuit filters and exchange rules apply to every trade, symbolic or not.

### Are brokerage charges the same during Muhurat trading?

Yes. Brokerage and statutory charges during the Muhurat session are the same as on a regular trading day under your normal brokerage plan. For reference, the standing charge figures on equity delivery trades are STT of 0.15% on the sell side, and NSE equity-options transaction charges of 0.03553% per side all-in. (Brokers may occasionally run one-off promos around the session, but the default rule is: normal plan, normal charges.)

### Is Muhurat trading guaranteed to be profitable?

No. The session carries no guarantee of returns, and press coverage has framed its significance as more traditional and symbolic than about immediate profitability — while the short trading window can heighten volatility. Any framing that treats the session as a sure-profit event is a myth. Trade only what you can afford to risk, and treat expectations the way you would on any ordinary trading day.

### What happens if I miss the Muhurat session?

Nothing bad. You miss the symbolic window, and that's all — there is no penalty and no missed opportunity in any structural sense. The next regular trading day is Monday, 9 November 2026, and everything works exactly as usual.

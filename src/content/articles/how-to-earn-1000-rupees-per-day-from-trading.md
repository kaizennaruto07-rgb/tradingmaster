To earn ₹1,000 a day from trading, you need ₹1,00,000 earning 1% every single day before costs — and no trader earns 1% every single day.

That one sentence is the whole answer. The rest of this article shows the working, because the working is what protects your capital.

## How to Earn ₹1,000 Per Day From Trading: The Math First

Daily profit is not a salary. It is a percentage of your capital, earned only on the days the market cooperates.

The formula is simple:

- Daily target ÷ capital = daily return you must earn
- ₹1,000 ÷ ₹10,000 = 10% a day
- ₹1,000 ÷ ₹50,000 = 2% a day
- ₹1,000 ÷ ₹1,00,000 = 1% a day

Every article that promises ₹1,000 a day without showing this table is hiding the price of admission. Now look at what those percentages actually demand.

### The 10% a day absurdity test

Take the smallest number first. Earning 10% a day on ₹10,000 sounds small because ₹1,000 sounds small.

Compounding exposes it. At 10% a day, ₹10,000 becomes about ₹67,275 in 20 trading days. Keep going for 60 trading days and it becomes roughly ₹30 lakh.

Nobody does this. Not beginners, not professionals, not anyone. If 10% a day were achievable, every small account would become a fortune within months, and the market would have no losers left to take the other side of those trades.

### Losing days are part of the math, not an exception

Generic articles show the winning day and stop there. Real months have both kinds of days.

Consider a trader with ₹50,000 who has a genuinely good month: 12 winning days of ₹1,000 and 8 losing days of ₹700.

- Gross wins: 12 × ₹1,000 = ₹12,000
- Gross losses: 8 × ₹700 = ₹5,600
- Net before costs: ₹6,400 for the month

That is ₹320 a day on average, not ₹1,000 — from a month most beginners would dream of. This is the first reason ₹1,000 a day is not a plan. It is a best-day outcome being sold as an everyday outcome.

## Can I Earn ₹1,000 Per Day From Trading?

Yes, on some days. No, not as reliable daily income. Both halves of that answer matter.

A trader with enough capital and skill will have many ₹1,000 days. That same trader will also have losing days, flat days, and days when the right decision is to not trade at all.

SEBI's own research has found that the large majority of individual intraday traders lose money. That finding is not about intelligence or effort. It is about what happens when small capital meets a daily income expectation: the expectation forces the risk-taking, and the risk-taking produces the losses.

So the honest framing is this: ₹1,000 a day is a possible result, not a promised income. Anyone selling it as the second thing is selling you something, not teaching you something.

## How Much Capital Is Needed to Make ₹1,000 Daily?

The minimum capital depends on what daily return you can realistically sustain — and "sustain" means across winning and losing days, after costs, for months.

- ₹10,000 needs 10% a day. Not a plan.
- ₹25,000 needs 4% a day. Extremely hard, before costs.
- ₹50,000 needs 2% a day. Very hard, before costs.
- ₹1,00,000 needs 1% a day. Hard but conceivable — for a skilled trader, on average, not daily.

Notice the last line carefully. Even at ₹1,00,000, the 1% is an average, not a daily guarantee. A realistic good month might average out to far less than 1% a day once losing days and costs are counted.

### You also need a buffer, not just the maths

Capital does two jobs. It generates the return, and it absorbs the drawdown.

Consider a trader with ₹1,00,000 who hits a 20% drawdown. The account is now ₹80,000. Suddenly 1% is only ₹800, and the ₹1,000 target now needs 1.25% a day from a smaller base.

This is how traders spiral. The target stays fixed while the capital shrinks, so the required return keeps rising, so the risk keeps rising. The buffer — extra capital beyond the bare minimum — is what stops that spiral.

A trader targeting ₹1,000 a day should think in terms of ₹1,00,000 plus a buffer, not ₹1,00,000 exactly.

## Can I Earn ₹1,000 Daily With ₹10,000?

No, not in any way that survives contact with reality. The maths alone rules it out: 10% a day, every day, is not a trading strategy. It is a lottery ticket with extra steps.

The usual objection is leverage. If borrowed margin lets you control a bigger position, surely ₹10,000 can earn like ₹50,000? This misunderstands what leverage does.

- Leverage increases the size of your position, not the quality of your decisions.
- A 2% move on a leveraged position still equals 10% on your own ₹10,000 — profit or loss.
- The loss arrives just as fast as the profit, and it arrives from the same account that cannot afford it.

Leverage does not reduce the return you need on your own money. It only makes the swings bigger. For a ₹10,000 account chasing ₹1,000 a day, leverage turns a slow bleed into a fast blow-up.

The realistic path with ₹10,000 is different, and it is covered later in this article: learn, paper trade, and treat the account as tuition, not income.

## How Much Profit Can I Make With ₹50,000?

With ₹50,000, ₹1,000 a day needs 2% a day. That is very hard before costs, and costs make it harder still.

A more honest way to think about ₹50,000 is monthly. Suppose a skilled trader manages a strong month of 3% to 5% on that capital.

- 3% of ₹50,000 = ₹1,500 for the month, roughly ₹75 per trading day on average.
- 5% of ₹50,000 = ₹2,500 for the month, roughly ₹125 per trading day on average.

Compare that with the ₹1,000-a-day dream: ₹20,000 a month, or 40% a month, every month. No professional trader sustains 40% a month.

The gap between ₹125 a day and ₹1,000 a day is not a gap in effort. It is a gap in reality.

This does not mean ₹50,000 is useless. It means ₹50,000 is a learning and compounding account, not a salary replacement. Profits of ₹1,500 to ₹2,500 in a good month, reinvested and compounded over years while skill grows, is how small accounts actually become big ones.

## How to Size a Trade Toward a Daily Goal Without Blowing Up

If you still want to aim for a daily number, aim at it with rules that keep you alive. Here is the step-by-step method.

### Step 1: Fix your risk per trade before you think about profit

Risk no more than 1% of your capital on any single trade.

- On ₹50,000, that is ₹500 per trade.
- On ₹1,00,000, that is ₹1,000 per trade.

This is the number that decides whether you survive. A trader risking 1% can lose ten trades in a row and still have roughly 90% of the account. A trader risking 10% is finished after a normal bad week.

Use a position size calculator to convert that rupee risk into an actual quantity. Guesswork here is how traders accidentally risk five times what they intended. See the position size tool at /tools/position-size/ and the position sizing guide at /articles/position-sizing/.

### Step 2: Demand twice what you risk

To make ₹1,000 while risking ₹500, you need trades that pay 2:1 — ₹1,000 of profit for ₹500 of risk.

That single rule changes everything about trade selection. Most random trades do not offer 2:1. So most of the time, the correct action is no trade.

- Entry only when the setup offers at least twice the stop distance as profit potential.
- If the setup offers 1:1, skip it. A 1:1 trade needs a win rate above 50% just to break even before costs.
- Two winning 2:1 trades at ₹500 risk = ₹2,000 gross. One losing trade = ₹500 gone. The maths works only when the filter stays strict.

### Step 3: Cap your trades and your daily loss

Set these three limits in writing before the market opens:

- Maximum 3 trades per day. More trades mean more costs and more emotional decisions, not more profit.
- Daily loss limit of 2% of capital. On ₹50,000, stop trading for the day at −₹1,000. On ₹1,00,000, stop at −₹2,000.
- No trading after the loss limit is hit. No "one more trade to recover it". That trade is how accounts die.

Consider a trader with ₹50,000 following these rules. On a good day, two 2:1 winners make ₹2,000 gross. On a bad day, the loss stops at ₹1,000.

The asymmetry is the whole strategy: capped downside, uncapped patience.

### Step 4: Review weekly, never daily

A daily target makes you judge yourself every evening. A weekly review makes you judge your process.

- Every weekend, log: trades taken, rules followed, rules broken, net result.
- If you followed the rules and lost money, the process stays. Results follow process over months, not days.
- If you broke the rules and made money, that is still a failure. Rewarded rule-breaking is how the next blow-up gets built.

Read how losing streaks damage accounts at /articles/how-to-size-positions-after-a-losing-streak/ and how ruin actually works at /articles/risk-of-ruin-trading/. Both explain why the daily loss limit matters more than any entry signal.

## The Contrarian Truth: The ₹1,000 Target Is the Problem

Here is the insight generic articles miss. A fixed daily rupee target does not motivate good trading. It manufactures bad trading.

The mechanism is simple and brutal:

- On slow days, the market offers nothing worth trading. The target does not care. So the trader trades anyway.
- A forced trade is usually a low-quality trade. Low-quality trades lose more often.
- The loss puts the trader behind target. Behind target, the trader increases size or takes a second, worse trade to "catch up".
- That is revenge trading wearing a productivity costume.

Now flip it. Replace the rupee target with process targets you fully control:

- Take at most 3 trades, only on setups that offer 2:1 or better.
- Risk 1% per trade, no exceptions.
- Stop at the daily loss limit, no exceptions.
- Review every weekend, no exceptions.

A process target can be hit on a day the market gives nothing — by taking zero trades, you followed the process perfectly. A rupee target cannot be hit on such a day without breaking rules. That is why process targets beat rupee targets: they reward discipline on the exact days when discipline is hardest.

## Can Beginners Earn Daily From Trading?

No — not while learning, and the learning phase is longer than anyone selling a course admits.

A beginner's first months have a different job: don't lose the account while building skill. Daily income is not the job yet. Expect this sequence instead:

- **Months 1–2:** Learn the basics and paper trade. Paper trading means practising with virtual money on real market prices. It costs nothing and reveals whether your strategy even works.
- **Months 3–6:** Trade tiny real capital with strict rules. Expect small losses and small wins. The goal is rule-following, not profit.
- **After that:** Scale up only after several consistent months. Consistency means months, not a lucky week.

Beginners who skip this sequence usually arrive at the same place: an account small enough that ₹1,000 a day needs an impossible percentage, traded with leverage, until a normal losing streak ends it. The market charges tuition either way. Paper trading charges it in time.

Real trading with a daily target charges it in capital.

## Is ₹1,000 Daily Trading Income Guaranteed?

No. Plainly and completely: no.

Trading income is never guaranteed, for beginners or experts. Markets do not pay salaries. They pay a distribution of outcomes, and any single day can land anywhere in that distribution.

Three consequences follow:

- Anyone promising guaranteed daily profits from trading is selling something, not teaching something. Walk away.
- A strategy that worked last month can lose this month. That is normal, not proof the strategy is broken — but it is proof the income was never guaranteed.
- If you need ₹1,000 a day to pay bills, trading cannot be that source yet. Build the capital and the skill first, with other income covering your living costs.

Repeat this before every session: the market owes you nothing today. Your only job is to follow the rules and survive to trade tomorrow.

## How Much Do Brokerage and Taxes Reduce Trading Profits?

More than beginners expect, and the damage is worst exactly where the profits are smallest. Here is a worked example using real charge figures.

Consider a delivery trade: buy shares worth ₹50,000, later sell them for ₹51,000.

- Gross profit: ₹1,000.
- Securities Transaction Tax (STT) on delivery is 0.1% on both sides: ₹50 on the buy, ₹51 on the sell — ₹101 total.
- Net after STT alone: ₹899, before brokerage, exchange charges, GST, stamp duty, and SEBI charges, which all add further costs.

So on a ₹1,000 gross profit, at least ₹101 — more than 10% — is gone to one tax alone. On a smaller gross profit the bite is proportionally worse: a ₹300 gross day can lose a third or more of itself to charges before you count anything else.

Options traders face their own version: STT of 0.15% applies on the sell side, including on exercised in-the-money options, plus the NSE equity-options charge of 0.03553% per side. The percentages look tiny until you multiply them by daily turnover across many trades.

And then there is tax on the profit itself. Trading profits are taxable in India, and the treatment differs by segment and holding period. Keep complete trade records and consult a chartered accountant rather than guessing — an article cannot settle your tax position, and a wrong guess here costs real money.

The practical lesson: costs are a fixed headwind. A trader needs a bigger edge than the costs, every month, just to stand still. Small accounts chasing small daily profits feel this headwind hardest, which is one more reason ₹1,000 a day needs a bigger base than the headline maths suggests.

## A Realistic Plan Instead of a Daily Target

Drop the daily target. Replace it with this plan:

- **Build capital first.** Save and add to the account from other income. Capital is what turns a realistic percentage into meaningful rupees.
- **Measure monthly, not daily.** A month of 3% to 5% on growing capital is a genuine achievement. Judge yourself on months and quarters.
- **Cap risk always.** 1% per trade, 2% daily loss limit, 3 trades maximum. These numbers are your real edge while skill catches up.
- **Journal every trade.** The traders who improve are the ones who can show, in writing, what they did and why.
- **Reinvest profits.** Let compounding do the heavy lifting over years instead of demanding it daily.

₹1,000 a day may arrive eventually — as a byproduct of a bigger account and a tested process. It arrives for traders who stopped chasing it daily and built the base instead. That is the honest path, and it is the only one that does not end with a blown account.

## Intraday or Swing: Which Route Fits a Daily Goal?

Neither route makes ₹1,000 a day reliable. They just fail differently.

Intraday trading — buying and selling within the same day — gives you a result every evening. That is exactly why it appeals to daily-target traders. It is also why it is dangerous for them.

- Every intraday trade adds costs, and costs compound against small profits.
- Every intraday session adds decisions, and decisions under a daily target get emotional by afternoon.
- A missed target at 2 pm becomes a forced trade at 2:30 pm. That trade is usually the worst one of the day.

Swing trading — holding for days or weeks — removes the daily pressure. A swing trader does not need today to pay. A good week can average out to a strong daily figure without any single day being forced.

- Fewer trades mean fewer costs eating the profit.
- Fewer decisions mean fewer emotional errors.
- The trade-off: income arrives in lumps, not daily. A swing trader can go a week without booking anything.

Consider a trader with a day job and ₹50,000. Intraday trading demands full market hours and daily emotional discipline. Swing trading needs an hour in the evening for review and planning.

For most beginners, swing fits life better — and a style that fits your life is one you can actually sustain for the years compounding needs.

The verdict: if the goal is truly daily cash, neither style guarantees it. If the goal is building toward ₹1,000 days, swing trading's lower cost and lower pressure give beginners a better survival rate. Survival is the prerequisite everything else depends on.

## What a Real ₹1,000 Day Actually Looks Like

Consider a trader with ₹1,00,000 who follows the rules in this article. The day starts with a plan, not a hope.

- Trade one: a setup offering 2:1. Risk ₹1,000, target ₹2,000. The stop hits. Loss: ₹1,000.
- Trade two: another 2:1 setup later in the session. Risk ₹1,000, target ₹2,000. The target hits. Profit: ₹2,000.
- Net before costs: ₹1,000. After charges, a little less.

Notice what made the day work. Two trades, not ten. Fixed risk, not growing size.

And a losing trade first — the day was red before it was green.

A trader chasing ₹1,000 would have doubled size after the first loss. This trader did not, and that restraint is the entire difference.

Also notice what the day did not include: no third trade to "make it ₹2,000", no holding into the close hoping for more. The target was met, so trading stopped. Quitting while ahead is a skill, not a weakness.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I earn ₹1,000 per day from trading?

On some days, yes — with enough capital and skill, ₹1,000 days happen regularly. As reliable daily income, no.

Markets have losing days and flat days, and no trader earns the same amount every day. Treat ₹1,000 as a possible result, never as a promised salary.

### How much capital is needed to make ₹1,000 daily?

At least ₹1,00,000, and realistically more once you add a buffer for drawdowns. ₹1,00,000 needs 1% a day before costs; ₹50,000 needs 2% a day; ₹10,000 needs 10% a day. The smaller the capital, the more impossible the daily percentage becomes, and costs make every tier harder than the headline maths suggests.

### Can I earn ₹1,000 daily with ₹10,000?

No. ₹1,000 on ₹10,000 is 10% a day, every day — a rate that would turn ₹10,000 into roughly ₹67,275 in 20 trading days and about ₹30 lakh in 60 trading days. Nobody sustains that.

Leverage does not fix it either: it only makes the same percentage swings bigger, losses included. With ₹10,000, treat trading as learning, not income.

### How much profit can I make with ₹50,000?

A strong month of 3% to 5% gives ₹1,500 to ₹2,500 for the month — roughly ₹75 to ₹125 per trading day on average. That is the honest range for a skilled month, not ₹1,000 a day.

₹1,000 a day on ₹50,000 means 40% a month, every month, which no professional sustains. Use ₹50,000 to learn, compound, and build toward a bigger base.

### Can beginners earn daily from trading?

No, not while learning. Beginners should expect months of small losses and small wins while they build skill and rule-following habits.

Paper trade first, then trade tiny real capital with strict risk limits, and scale up only after several consistent months. Daily income is a late-stage outcome, not a starting point.

### Is ₹1,000 daily trading income guaranteed?

No, never — for beginners or experts. Trading pays a distribution of outcomes, not a salary, and any day can be a losing day.

Anyone promising guaranteed daily profits is selling something, not teaching something. If you need ₹1,000 a day to cover living costs, trading cannot be that source yet.

### How much do brokerage and taxes reduce trading profits?

Significantly, especially on small profits. On a ₹50,000 delivery buy sold for ₹51,000, the ₹1,000 gross profit loses ₹101 to delivery STT alone (0.1% on both sides), leaving ₹899 before brokerage, exchange charges, GST, stamp duty, and SEBI charges. Options traders additionally face 0.15% STT on the sell side plus the NSE equity-options charge of 0.03553% per side.

Trading profits are also taxable, so keep full records and consult a chartered accountant.

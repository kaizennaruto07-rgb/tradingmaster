*By Kaizen · 24 October 2026 · Monks Of Market · Muhurat Trading Series (Part 3 of 4)*

Here's the question every first-timer asks before their first Diwali session: **"Should I actually buy?"** It's a fair question, and it deserves an honest answer — not a festive sales pitch.

Here is the honest answer in one line: **yes, you can buy — but only if you treat the session as a beginning, not a trade.** Auspicious is not a strategy. Intention is.

This guide is for first-time investors and beginner traders (zero to one year of market exposure) facing their first Muhurat session. Think of it as muhurat trading tips for first-time investors — the practical kind: a concrete decision protocol, a pre-session checklist, exactly what you may and may not buy, and how much to risk. And if you decide to do nothing at all — that is also a legitimate answer.

## The Honest Answer: Yes — If You Treat It as a Beginning, Not a Trade

Let's settle the main question first: **can beginners participate in Muhurat trading?** Yes. There is no experience requirement. The session is open to any retail investor with an active Demat and trading account.

But "you can participate" is not the same as "you should buy." The right frame is this: Muhurat trading is a ritual that marks the start of the Hindu financial year — a symbolic first trade for good beginnings. It is not a trading opportunity. The calendar gives you no systematic edge, the session creates no special, systematic returns, and nothing about the hour makes a stock more worth buying than it was last week.

So the question is not really "should I buy stocks on Muhurat trading?" It's **"should I buy stocks — and is the Muhurat session a reasonable moment to do it?"** If the answer to the first part is yes — you have a pre-researched stock or ETF you intended to hold long term — then the session is as good a moment as any. If the answer is no, the session doesn't change it.

## What Muhurat Trading Is, in 60 Seconds

Muhurat trading is a special short trading session held on Diwali (Lakshmi Pujan), marking the start of the new Samvat year — about 70 minutes of trading. For 2026: **Sunday, November 8, 2026** — Diwali — welcoming **Samvat 2083**.

The main trading window runs **2:20–3:30 PM IST** (per the official NSE circular NSE/CMTR/76941, Ref 149/2026, and BSE notice 20261019-9, both dated 19 October 2026). Note: this is an afternoon session, not the evening session of years past — if you read a page that still says "approximately 1 hour (evening)" or "timings to be notified," it is stale. The full official schedule — block deals, pre-open, and post-close phases — is covered in [the full official schedule](/articles/muhurat-trading-2026/).

The tradition goes back decades — the first official session ran in 1957. For [the full 1957-to-today story](/articles/muhurat-trading-history/), see our history article. For this article, only one thing matters: it's a ritual of beginnings, and you're a beginner. That fits.

Notice what the protocol does: it moves the decision from *the session* to *your readiness*. The session is just a date. Your readiness is the actual trade.

One note for the true first-timer: **if you have neither a pre-researched stock nor an ETF you already planned to buy, the protocol's answer for you this year is watch — that is the correct, intended outcome**, not a fallback.

### The Pre-Session Checklist: Six Boxes to Tick Before You Click Buy

Before you place any order, tick all six. If even one is missing, the honest answer is "watch this year, buy next year."

1. **Account ready.** Active Demat + trading account with a SEBI-registered broker, KYC complete (PAN, Aadhaar, bank account linked), and funds already in the trading account. Opening an account a day or two before Diwali may not complete in time — don't discover this at 2:20 PM on a Sunday.
2. **Funds settled and small.** The amount is decided in advance, transferred in advance, and small relative to your capital.
3. **One pre-researched pick.** Exactly one stock or ETF — already on your own watchlist, researched by *you*, intended as a long-term holding. Not a tip, not a "Muhurat picks" list, not something you heard about this morning.
4. **Limit order decided.** You know the price you're willing to pay, and you'll place a limit order at or below it. In a thin session, limit orders are the safer default (see the risks section for why); a market order may fill at a worse price than you expected.
5. **Position sized.** You've worked out how much this purchase is as a fraction of your total capital, and it is small. (More on this below.)
6. **Journal page open.** You know exactly what you'll write down after the trade — price, quantity, reason, and the holding thesis. See the journal section below.

## What a Beginner May Legitimately Buy — and What to Skip

**What should beginners buy during Muhurat trading?** Broad categories only — this article names no stocks and gives no picks, because a pick you didn't research is just a tip with better branding.

- **A quality long-term holding already on your watchlist.** One company you understand, whose business you could explain in two sentences, that you intended to hold for years. A single share counts. The ceremony doesn't require size.
- **One broad index ETF from your own watchlist.** For many first-timers, this is the most honest choice: instant diversification, no single-company research burden, and a holding you were going to make anyway.

Both share the same test: **would you buy this exact thing on a random Tuesday in February?** If yes, the Muhurat session is a fine moment. If the only reason is "it's Diwali," skip it.

**What to skip:** anything you haven't researched, anything bought because a list or a tip said so, anything speculative you wouldn't hold through a normal month. And anything leveraged or derivative — the next section is non-negotiable.

## How Much to Put In: Small, Sized, and Survivable

**Can you start Muhurat trading with a small amount?** Yes. A single share is a completely legitimate symbolic start. The ritual marks a beginning; it has no minimum ticket size.

To make "small" concrete — this is illustration, not advice: for some beginners that might be a single share worth a few hundred rupees. What counts is not the rupee figure but the proportion: the amount must be small relative to your total capital.

**How much of your capital should go into one stock as a beginner?** Think in terms of survival, not returns. Beginner guides commonly suggest keeping any single stock to a small, single-digit slice of total capital — a 5–10% cap, say. Treat that as guidance, not a rule: the principle is that no single purchase, Muhurat or otherwise, should be able to meaningfully damage your capital.

Two sizing principles that matter more than any number:

1. **Size it as if the session were any normal day.** Do not raise your bet because the occasion feels special. The festival doesn't change the risk; it only changes your emotions.
2. **Size it so that losing half wouldn't change your sleep.** If a 50% fall in this purchase would genuinely hurt, it's too big. This is a ritual purchase, not a wealth plan.

## The Honest Math: the Session Is Symbolic in Scale

Here is the part the festive marketing won't tell you: **the session's gains are symbolic in scale.** Seventy minutes of trading — however auspicious — does not compound into wealth. Your portfolio in February will not remember the 70 minutes you bought; it will remember *what* you bought and *why*.

This is arithmetic, not a data claim: the point doesn't depend on what any particular session returned, only on the arithmetic of what one small purchase can and can't do.

This is worth stating plainly because some pages quote session-return averages or historical win-rate statistics ("X of the past Y years, indices gained"). Those numbers, even when accurate, answer a question you shouldn't be asking. A beginner buying one small long-term holding does not experience the session's average — they experience one stock, bought once, held for years. The calendar gives no edge. The only thing that compounds is buying something worth holding and then holding it.

So when someone asks whether the gains are "real": the trades are real, the prices are real, the money is real. But there is no Muhurat premium. The value of participating is in the beginning it marks — the discipline, the ritual, the first entry in the journal — not in the session's returns.

## What Beginners Must NOT Do During Muhurat Trading

These are the hard guardrails — the traps that turn a ritual into a loss.

- **No F&O. Especially not in your first year.** Options and futures are leveraged: small price moves become large losses. A thin festival session plus leverage is the worst beginner combination there is. Beginner guides uniformly advise staying away from derivatives until you have real experience.
- **No intraday speculation.** The session is 70 minutes of trading. Trying to scalp moves in a short, thin session is not a strategy; it's a lottery ticket with brokerage attached.
- **No tips.** If you didn't research it, you don't buy it. A recommendation you can't explain is a position you can't manage.
- **No oversized bets.** Nothing about Diwali makes a large position safer. Keep it small, sized, and survivable (see above).
- **No "special occasion" sizing.** The most dangerous sentence in investing is "just this once, because it's Muhurat." Size it like a Tuesday.

Learn to spot the patterns:

- **The picks list.** A broker sends "top 10 Muhurat picks." Ask: is this research or marketing? If you hadn't researched these names before the list arrived, they're not your picks.
- **The WhatsApp tip.** Forwards promising festive returns are a documented scam and pump vector around Diwali. The industry itself warns about them. The response is always the same: ignore.
- **The urgency.** "Buy before the session starts!" Urgency is how FOMO bypasses your checklist. A real long-term holding will still be buyable on Monday, next month, and next Diwali.

And turn the defense into a behaviour rule, not just something you've read: **on session day, open the broker app only to place your one planned limit order, then close it.** Don't browse, don't scroll the picks lists, don't "just look" at what's moving.

If you want to understand the psychology behind this, read [how festive FOMO works](/articles/fomo-trading/) — the Diwali variant is the same machinery in festival clothes.

## Risks and Limitations: What a Thin Festival Session Really Means

Honest risk disclosure, no sugarcoating:

- **Your capital is at risk.** An order executed during Muhurat trading is a real trade with real settlement obligations and real losses possible. (Standard charges apply exactly as on a normal day — see the practical steps below.)
- **The session can be thin.** Brokers caution that short festival sessions can see lower liquidity than a normal day. In a thin session, prices can move more erratically than usual, especially in small, less-liquid names — one more reason to stick to quality holdings or broad ETFs and keep size small.
- **Limit orders may go unfilled.** In a thin session, your limit price may simply never get hit. That's the trade-off for price protection — and it's acceptable. An unfilled order is not a loss. The beginner rule here: place one limit order and walk away; do not re-place or chase.
- **Market orders may fill at worse prices.** This is why the checklist defaults to limit orders: in lower liquidity, a market order can execute further from the price you saw on screen.
- **The ritual is not a hedge.** Nothing about the auspiciousness protects your capital. Risk of ruin applies to small symbolic trades too — if you want the math behind why survival comes before returns, see [risk of ruin](/articles/risk-of-ruin-trading/).

## Watching Is a Valid Option: You're Allowed to Observe

This deserves its own section because beginners rarely hear it: **you are allowed to watch the entire session and buy nothing.** That is a valid first participation — and for many first-timers, it's the best one.

Watching productively means watching with intent:

- **Watch the order flow.** See how a limit order sits in the book, how a market order fills, what the pre-open does to prices. This is free education.
- **Watch price discovery.** Notice how prices move when fewer participants are in the market (see the risks section above). You'll feel, in real time, why the checklist insists on limit orders.
- **Watch yourself.** Notice the itch to click buy because everyone else seems to be. That itch is the most valuable thing you'll observe all afternoon — it's the feeling your checklist exists to manage.

Next year, you'll participate with a watched session behind you. That's not a year wasted; that's a year of tuition you didn't pay for.

## The Practical Steps If You Decide to Buy

If the protocol says "buy," here's the practical flow on Sunday, November 8, 2026:

1. **Before the session:** log in to your broker's app, confirm funds are in the trading account, and pull up your one pre-researched stock or ETF. Decide your limit price in advance — not in the moment.
2. **During the session (2:20–3:30 PM IST):** place a single limit order at your decided price. One order, one purchase. Do not chase if it doesn't fill — an unfilled limit order in a thin session is a normal outcome, not a failure.
3. **After the session:** confirm the order status in the app. Note that standard charges apply on the trade, exactly as on a normal day — the detailed charges and settlement breakdown is covered in our [settlement article](/articles/muhurat-trading-settlement-charges/).
4. **If your account isn't ready:** don't rush KYC or fund transfers on the day. As noted in the checklist, opening an account days before Diwali may not complete in time — and a rushed account opening is a bad first trade before you've even traded. Watch this year instead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should beginners participate in Muhurat trading?

Yes — as a beginning, not a trade. A small, planned, pre-researched long-term purchase, or simply watching the session, are both legitimate answers. What's not legitimate for a beginner: F&O, tips, intraday speculation, and oversized bets.

### Is Muhurat trading good for beginners?

It's good for discipline and ritual — a meaningful moment to start a long-term investing habit with intention. It's not good for learning to "trade": 70 minutes of a thin session teaches you nothing about trading skill, and treating it as a trading opportunity is how beginners get hurt.

### What should a beginner buy during Muhurat trading?

Broad categories only: a quality long-term holding already on your own watchlist, or one broad index ETF you had already planned to buy. Never tips, never a "Muhurat picks" list, never anything you can't explain in two sentences. If you'd buy it on a random Tuesday in February, it's a fine Muhurat purchase.

### Can I start Muhurat trading with a small amount?

Yes. A single share is a completely legitimate symbolic start. The ceremony marks a beginning — it has no minimum ticket size, and small is the correct size for a ritual purchase.

### How much of my capital should I put into one stock as a beginner?

Keep it small and sized: beginner guides commonly suggest capping any single stock at a small, single-digit slice of total capital — a 5–10% cap is a frequently cited figure, guidance, not a rule. The principle: no single purchase should be able to meaningfully damage your capital, and you should size it like any normal day. For the full framework, see our [position sizing guide](/articles/position-sizing/).

### Should beginners try F&O during Muhurat trading?

No — especially not in the first year. Options and futures are leveraged, and a thin festival session is the worst place to learn that leverage cuts both ways. Beginner guides uniformly advise staying away from derivatives until you have real experience. Buy the share or the ETF; leave the derivatives alone.

### Do I need a Demat account for Muhurat trading?

Yes. You need an active Demat account plus a trading account with a SEBI-registered broker, completed KYC (PAN, Aadhaar, linked bank account), and funds in the trading account. If you don't have these ready, open them after Diwali without rushing — and watch this year's session instead.

### Is Muhurat trading held every year?

Yes — it's an annual tradition held on Diwali (Lakshmi Pujan), tied to Chopda Pujan and the Hindu accounting new year. The first official BSE session ran in 1957. For [the full 1957-to-today story](/articles/muhurat-trading-history/), see our history article.

### Does Muhurat trading affect stock prices — and are the gains real?

The trades are real: real orders, real price discovery, real money, real settlement obligations. But there is no calendar edge — nothing about the session makes stocks systematically gain. Some pages quote historical session averages or win-rate statistics; those numbers answer a question you shouldn't be asking, because a beginner buying one small long-term holding experiences one stock, not an average. The honest math: the session is symbolic in scale.

### Should I buy on Diwali tips shared on WhatsApp?

No. Ignore them. Festive tip forwards are a documented promotional and scam vector around Diwali — the industry itself warns beginners against them. If a tip arrives, run it through the festive-FOMO defense above: not researched by you means not bought by you.

## The Takeaway: the Session Is a Beginning, Not a Strategy

So — should you actually buy? If your Demat is ready, your funds are in, you have one pre-researched long-term holding on your watchlist, your limit price is decided, and the amount is small enough to be symbolic: yes. Buy one small thing, journal it, and hold it.

If any of that isn't true: watch. Watch the order flow, watch the prices, watch your own itch to click buy. That's a valid first Diwali in the markets — arguably the smarter one.

Either way, remember the thesis: **auspicious is not a strategy — but intention is.** The session doesn't make you an investor. Deciding in advance what you'll do, sizing it so it can't hurt you, ignoring the noise, and writing it all down — that does. Samvat 2083 starts on November 8. Start it like someone who plans to still be here for Samvat 2084.
